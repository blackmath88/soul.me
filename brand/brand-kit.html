Why a fox 狐

Japanese has one word for a ghost and a shapeshifter: obake お化け, "a thing that changes". Its root is 化 (bakeru, to transform). That's the brand in one character: a self that stays itself while it keeps taking new forms.

Rules for the kit 掟

  1. Grey is the world; colour is yours. Vendors, employers, proposals, processes and the night are drawn in greys. Colour appears only on what you've sealed.
  2. A line changes colour at one moment only: the seal. Nothing automatic ever turns anything to colour.
  3. Shu is the human's hand. The seal, a stamped line, a review waiting for you. Nothing else is red.
  4. The face may change; the self doesn't. Borrowed masks are grey; the fox's body and its reflection keep their colour.
  5. Masks are tributes, not our marks. Always grey, never in vendor colours. In product surfaces, follow each provider's brand guidelines or use their official icon.
  6. Pictures illustrate state; they never replace a label. Every seal, tail and incense stick sits next to words.
  7. Scenes fade; working surfaces don't. Smoke, wisps and the centerpiece live in the README, landing, onboarding and empty states.
  8. Everything is generated. Marks, keepers and ornaments come from seeded rules and pixel grids, reviewed like code, same as the vault.

Design exploration, round 2: the fox, masks, parade and ornaments from the round-1 alternative kit, merged with the greys, shadow play and obake wisps from the first.
