Shapeshifting 変化

In the lore, bakeru means to transform, so an obake is by definition a shapeshifter. Ours moves through five forms and always settles back into the ghost. Every form keeps the same eyes in the same place: the shape changes, the self stays.

Palette 墨

Two old ideas carry the depth. Sumi-e says ink has five colours: one black, five densities. And Edo-period dyers, when commoners were restricted to plain colours, answered with "forty-eight browns and a hundred greys". So: no hue, but temperature (warm ink, cool lead), material (lacquer, ink, charcoal) and one reserved white. Traditional names, approximate values.

Rules for the kit 掟

  1. White is the human. Gofun appears only for the glint in the eyes, the seal of approval and the review slip. Nothing automatic is ever white.
  2. Tone carries provenance. Solid means stated, dither means imported, outline means inferred. Never swap them; always print the tag too.
  3. The ghost may change its shape, never its eyes. Every form keeps the same two eyes in the same place.
  4. Depth comes from tone, temperature and material, never hue. The one tinted grey (rikyū) is a nuance, not an accent.
  5. Scenes fade; working surfaces don't. Dissolving edges and shapeshifting belong to the landing page, onboarding and empty states.
  6. Everything is generated. Marks, icons and ornaments come from seeded rules and pixel grids, so they can be rebuilt and reviewed like code.

Design exploration, round 1. Every scene, mark, icon and ornament is drawn procedurally in greys: no images, no tracing. Colour names are traditional Japanese names with approximate values.
